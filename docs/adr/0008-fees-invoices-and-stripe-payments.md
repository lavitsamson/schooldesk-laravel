# ADR 0008: Fees as integer money, idempotent term invoices, and Stripe payments recorded by webhook only

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Date:** 2026-10-25
- **Deciders:** Lavit Samson

## Context

Schools charge fees per term (tuition), once a year (activities, copying) or once (an admission
fee). Each term, every enrolled student needs an invoice; parents pay online, by bank transfer
or in cash at the office, sometimes in parts; and the office has to see what is still open.
Several things make this harder than it looks:

- Money must add up to the cent. Floats do not (`0.1 + 0.2 !== 0.3`), and a school's books are
  checked against the bank statement.
- Invoices are generated by a scheduled job. Jobs run twice (a retry, two servers, an admin
  clicking "Generate now" while the nightly run is on), and a family must never get two
  invoices for one term.
- Invoice numbers end up in bank references and in bookkeeping. German rules (GoBD) expect
  them to be sequential without gaps.
- Card payments happen on Stripe's page, not ours. The browser coming back to a "success" URL
  proves nothing. Stripe's webhooks can arrive twice, out of order, or at the same time.
- Webhooks arrive without a session or a school context, but every record is school-scoped
  (ADR 0001).

## Decision

1. **Money is integers in minor units, wrapped in `App\Support\Money`.** Every amount column is
   an unsigned integer (cents). `Money` parses what people type (`1200`, `1.200,50`,
   `1,200.50`) digit by digit, adds and subtracts as integers, refuses to mix currencies, and
   formats per locale (`€1,200.50`, `1.200,50 €`) with intl. Only `format()` turns the amount
   into a number for the formatter, at the very edge. The frontend receives minor units and only
   formats them; the payment form is prefilled with a decimal string from the server.

2. **Fee structures per academic year, applied when invoices are generated.** A fee has an
   amount, a class (or every class) and a frequency:
   - `term`: on every term's invoice;
   - `annual`: once per student and year, left out when an earlier invoice that year has it;
   - `one_off`: once per student, and only in the student's first year at the school.

   Invoice items copy the fee's description and amount, so changing or deleting a fee never
   changes an issued invoice. The invoice keeps the class the student was in, for reporting.

3. **Generation is idempotent and safe to run concurrently.** `GenerateTermInvoices` skips
   students who already have an invoice for the term, and a unique index on
   `(student_id, term_id)` backs that up (a race ends in a caught unique violation, not a second
   invoice). A cache lock per school and term keeps the daily run and "Generate now" apart.
   `fees:generate-term-invoices` runs daily and invoices every term starting within 14 days, so
   later runs only fill gaps, such as a student enrolled last week.

4. **No draft state: generated invoices are issued.** The spec lists `draft` as a status, but
   a draft would need someone to review hundreds of invoices before they go out, which the
   daily automation is meant to avoid. Corrections are made by **voiding** an invoice without
   payments. Void is final, and the student is not invoiced for that term again (this is also
   how a scholarship is recorded). The statuses are `issued`, `partially_paid`, `paid` and
   `void`. "Overdue" is derived (open and past the due date), never stored, so it cannot go stale.

5. **Gapless invoice numbers per school and year** (`SD-2026-000123`). `NextInvoiceNumber`
   locks a counter row (`invoice_number_sequences`) and increments it inside the transaction
   that creates the invoice. If the invoice rolls back, so does the number. A voided invoice
   keeps its number.

6. **The paid amount is derived from payments, never stored on the invoice.** An invoice's paid
   amount is `sum(amount - refunded_amount)` of its payments. After every change to payments,
   `RecalculateInvoiceStatus` sets the status from total and paid, with the invoice row locked
   (`SELECT … FOR UPDATE`). Two admins recording cash at once therefore cannot overpay an
   invoice: the second one sees the first payment and gets a validation error.

   **A bank transfer is recorded once per school.** It needs its bank reference, which is
   stored normalised (upper case, no spaces) in `payments.bank_reference`, with a unique index
   on `(school_id, bank_reference)`. The form request names the invoice the transfer is already
   on, and the index stops two admins who pass that check at the same moment. The scope is the
   school, not the invoice, so the same transfer cannot land on a sibling's invoice either; two
   schools may of course see the same reference from their own banks. Cash payments keep an
   optional free-text note, which is not checked. Invoice numbers are already unique per
   school (decision 5).

7. **Stripe payments exist only when Stripe's webhook says so.**
   - `StartCheckout` creates a Checkout session for the open balance. It carries the school,
     invoice and user ids as metadata (also copied to the payment intent), and sends the invoice
     number and term but not the student's name (data minimisation). Nothing is recorded at this
     point, and the success page only says "being confirmed".
   - `POST /webhooks/stripe` sits outside the web middleware (no session, no CSRF token). The
     Stripe SDK verifies the signature over the raw body with a 5-minute tolerance against
     replays, before anything is read from it. Failures return 400.
   - **Idempotency, in one transaction:** the event id is claimed with an insert-or-ignore into
     `stripe_webhook_events`, then handled. A duplicate delivery finds the id taken (or waits for
     the first delivery to commit) and does nothing. If handling fails, the claim rolls back
     with it, and Stripe's retry processes the event again. Payments are additionally unique per
     `stripe_payment_intent_id`.
   - **Tenancy without a session:** for `checkout.session.completed`, the school comes from the
     signed metadata, and the invoice is then looked up *inside that school only*, so a forged
     or mistaken invoice id from another school is not found. For `charge.refunded`, the
     payment is found by its payment intent id. Stripe makes that id globally unique, so this
     one lookup runs without the school scope, and everything after it runs in the payment's
     school.
   - **A payment Stripe took is always recorded.** If the invoice was paid in cash in the
     meantime, it shows a credit, and the office refunds the difference in Stripe.
     `charge.refunded` carries the running refunded total, so a late or replayed event can
     never refund twice.
   - `payment_intent.payment_failed` is logged. The invoice stays open, and Stripe's Checkout
     page has already shown the parent the error.
   - Without `STRIPE_SECRET`, online payment is switched off (no button, a 422 if called), and
     the school records bank and cash payments only. The demo works without a Stripe account.

8. **Receipts are rendered once, in a queued job.** `GeneratePaymentReceipt` stores the receipt
   PDF on the private disk and then tells the primary guardian. A receipt downloaded before
   the job ran is rendered on the spot. Invoice PDFs, on the other hand, are rendered on every
   download, because they show the current payments and balance. Both are served only through
   temporary signed links and authorised again by `InvoicePolicy`.

9. **Notifications go to the primary guardian**, the family's contact (as for absences): a new
   invoice, a payment received, and overdue reminders 3 and 10 days after the due date. Each
   reminder is claimed under a row lock (`reminders_sent`) before it is sent, so a rerun never
   mails twice. A day the scheduler missed is caught up, but an invoice found 10 days late gets
   only the second reminder, not both.

10. **Who may do what.** School admins (`fees.manage`) manage fees, invoices and manual
    payments. Parents see and pay the invoices of their own children, through their guardian
    records. Teachers see nothing. Another school's invoice, PDF or receipt is a 404, even with a
    valid signature (tenant isolation suite).

## Consequences

- The fees dashboard aggregates in the database (one grouped query with a payments subquery),
  so it costs the same for 40 invoices or 4,000.
- An overpaid invoice is shown as a credit, and the dashboard counts it as collected, but never
  as negative outstanding.
- Refunds are made in the Stripe dashboard, not in SchoolDesk. Cash refunds are out of scope
  for v1.
- The demo seed dates its invoices back to two weeks before the autumn term and marks the
  reminders of overdue invoices as sent, so the demo scheduler does not mail every demo family.
- A school that wants to review invoices before they go out cannot do so in v1. If that is
  needed, a per-school "review first" setting can introduce a draft state later without
  changing the rest.
- Translations of the PDFs and emails, and activity logging of payments and voids, come with
  the localisation and audit milestone (M10).

## Alternatives considered

- **Decimal columns and bcmath (as for grades, ADR 0006).** This works, but money only ever
  needs whole cents and addition. Integers are simpler, faster, and the same on MySQL and
  PostgreSQL.
- **A `paid_amount` column on the invoice.** It would save a subquery, but it is a second
  source of truth that can drift from the payments (a refund handled in one place but not the
  other).
- **Record the payment on the Checkout success redirect.** This is faster for the parent, but
  anyone can open that URL, and a parent who closes the tab after paying would never be
  recorded. The webhook is the only reliable signal.
- **Laravel Cashier.** Cashier is built for subscriptions of a billable user. Here a family pays
  one-off invoices owned by a school, so the Stripe SDK with Checkout in payment mode is the
  smaller fit.
- **`MAX(number) + 1` for invoice numbers.** It is racy without a lock, and with a lock on the
  invoices table it blocks far more than a single counter row.
